## 4. DESIGN DIRECTION

The kit must look like a premium beauty or fashion brand campaign, **not** a default AI-generated corporate deck. Apply one direction consistently.

### Option A: "Noir Atelier" (default)
- **Feel:** luxury fashion-magazine cover. Dark, dramatic, image-led.
- **Palette:** near-black `#0E0E10`, champagne gold `#C8A96A`, blush `#E8CFC4`, off-white `#F6F2EC`.
- **Type:** high-contrast editorial serif for headlines (Playfair Display or Cormorant). Clean sans for body (Inter). If fonts are unavailable, use Cambria/Georgia and Calibri/Arial.
- **Layout:** full-bleed photographs with large headline overlays, thin gold rules, oversized chapter numerals (01–12), small-caps labels, cropped arch or circle image frames. Alternate dark photo spreads with light text pages.

### Option B: "Soft Studio"
- **Feel:** bright, airy, friendly lifestyle brand.
- **Palette:** white `#FFFFFF`, soft sage `#A9B8A4`, peach `#F4C9B5`, deep green `#243B35`.
- **Layout:** large rounded photo cards, colour-blocked panels, sticker-style callouts.

### Rules for either option
- 16:9 for the deck. Short copy. Strong hierarchy. Generous whitespace.
- Vary composition slide to slide. No repeating rows of identical cards. Do not use the same layout with a heading and three boxes on every slide.
- Do not use accent lines under titles, or decorative side stripes.
- Maximum text readable in 5–10 seconds per slide.
- Contrast and accessibility must be good. No thin, low-contrast text.
- Logo placement: clean, on a tile if the background is busy. Never stretch or recolour it.
- Apply a consistent photo colour grade to all images so licensed stock photos look like one shoot.

## 7. SLIDE STRUCTURE (12 SLIDES)

Each slide includes speaker notes in this form: **Say** (a short natural explanation), **Ask** (one or two discovery questions), **Show** (the relevant screen or demo). Invite the owner into the conversation from slide 2 onward.

**1. Cover.** "Your Salon. Your Style. Your System." / Digital experiences and custom software built around the way your salon works. Full-bleed salon photograph, minimal text, logo.

**2. The client experience begins before the client walks in.** Journey: Discover → Explore services → Choose a stylist → Book → Visit → Return. Ask: "How do new clients currently discover your salon and book their first appointment?"

**3. Great service takes focus. Operations should support it.** Possible challenges (never assumed): appointments across calls, messages and notebooks; last-minute changes and missed follow-ups; client preferences spread across places; hard-to-coordinate staff schedules; repeated questions on services and availability; limited visibility into bookings; a social presence that doesn't lead to booking. Line: "Every salon is different. The first step is finding out which of these actually affects yours." Ask: "Which part of managing appointments or client communication takes the most time?"

**4. From first click to loyal client.** Fragmented flow versus connected flow. Potential only: clearer path to booking, easier service information, more organised appointments, consistent confirmations and follow-ups where configured. Do not promise that no-shows disappear or revenue rises.

**5. The right tools for the way you work.** Five areas, shown editorially, not as a grid of 20 cards: Digital Presence; Bookings & Appointments; Client & Team Management; Engagement & Insights; Custom Integrations. State that these are possible capabilities, with final scope set in discovery.

**6. Designed to feel like your brand.** Large, readable illustrative screens (home, booking, dashboard), labelled "Illustrative concept".

**7. More clarity behind the chair.** Four outcomes: less scattered coordination; a smoother booking experience; more organised client context; better operational visibility. Line: "The goal is not more software. It is a better-fitting way to manage the work that matters." Ask: "If one part of your salon's day could become simpler, what would you choose?"

**8. Start with what you need. Grow from there.** Package comparison (see section 8): Essential, Professional, Custom, plus add-ons. Stepped or editorial layout, not generic SaaS pricing cards. Mark: "Proposed structure. Final scope confirmed after consultation."

**9. Add what you need, when you need it.** Add-ons (WhatsApp reminders, online payments, loyalty or membership, mobile app, advanced reports, extra branches) and how third-party charges are shown separately.

**10. From your salon's workflow to a working solution.** Six steps: 01 Understand, 02 Identify, 03 Design, 04 Build, 05 Test & Launch, 06 Support & Grow. Key line: "We don't ask your salon to change everything just to fit a generic system." Use a distinctive progression, not six identical boxes.

**11. Scope first. Then a clear proposal.** Commercial model (see section 8), with price placeholders only.

**12. Let's start with your salon.** FREE SALON DIGITALIZATION CONSULTATION + TAILORED DEMO. "No obligation / Based on your workflow / Practical and personalised." CTA: **Book Your Free Consultation**. Footer: brand, descriptor, `[Insert verified website]`, `[Insert verified email / phone / QR code]`. Strong closing photograph. ## 10. SALES GUIDE

- **Meeting flow:** welcome → discover → show relevant screens → discuss fit (Essential, Professional or Custom) → agree the next step.
- **Discovery questions (select 2–3 per meeting, never all):**
  - How do most new clients find your salon today?
  - Do clients book by phone, WhatsApp, social media, website or walk-in?
  - How do you manage stylist availability and appointment changes?
  - What do you keep about returning clients?
  - How do you handle reminders and follow-ups?
  - One location or several? Which tools should stay?
  - What is the one operational issue you most want to improve?
- **Objection answers:** "We use a notebook" (ask what works and what takes time); "Is it expensive?" (scope first, then a clear proposal); "We're too small" (start with Essential); "Will it replace our tools?" (only if you want, we can integrate).
- Do not force a package early. Lead with the salon's problems, then fit the package.
